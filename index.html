What is Domain Rating (DR)?

Domain Rating is a score from 0 to 100, created by Ahrefs, that shows how strong a website’s backlink profile is compared with every other website on the internet. A backlink is a link from another website to yours. The more unique, trusted websites link to a site, the higher its DR.

SEOs, marketers and founders use DR to size up a website quickly: before buying a domain, choosing where to guest post, judging a link-building opportunity, comparing yourself with competitors, or tracking whether your own site is gaining authority over time.

What is a good Domain Rating?

DR is relative, so “good” depends on your niche and your competitors. As a rough guide:

DRLevelWhat it usually means
0–9Just startingNew sites with few links from other websites.
10–29SproutingEarly traction: a handful of good backlinks.
30–49GrowingA solid, growing site with real links.
50–69EstablishedA well-known site in its niche.
70–89AuthorityMajor brands, publishers and big platforms.
90–100EliteThe strongest sites on the web.

How is Domain Rating calculated?

Ahrefs crawls the web and, for every website, looks at three things:

  1. How many unique websites link to it

    Ten links from ten different websites count far more than a hundred links from one.

  2. How strong those websites are

    A link from a high-DR site passes more strength than one from a brand-new blog.

  3. How many sites each of them links to

    A site that links to only a few others passes more to each one than a site that links out to thousands.

Only followed (dofollow) links count. The result is placed on a 0–100 scale that gets harder to climb the higher you go: moving from DR 10 to 20 is much easier than moving from 70 to 80.

How to increase your Domain Rating

  1. Publish things people want to cite

    Original data, studies, guides and templates attract links on their own, month after month.

  2. Build a free tool

    Calculators, checkers and generators earn links from blogs and resource pages.

  3. Write guest posts on relevant sites

    One link from a respected site in your niche beats many from unrelated ones.

  4. Get listed in quality directories

    Pick curated, relevant directories that give followed links, not link farms.

  5. Reclaim lost and unlinked mentions

    Ask sites that mention your brand, or used to link to you, to add or restore the link.

  6. Track it and be patient

    DR responds to new links over weeks, not days. Watching the trend shows what works.

Domain Rating vs Domain Authority

Domain Rating (DR) is Ahrefs’ score; Domain Authority (DA) is Moz’s; Semrush has Authority Score. All three estimate the strength of a site’s backlinks on a 0–100 scale, but each uses its own link index and formula, so the same site gets different numbers. None of them is used by Google directly. Pick one, use it consistently, and compare sites with the same metric.

Frequently asked questions

What is Domain Rating (DR)?

Domain Rating is a score from 0 to 100, made by Ahrefs, that shows how strong a website’s backlink profile is compared with every other website. The more strong, unique websites link to a site, the higher its DR.

What is a good Domain Rating?

It depends on your niche. Most new sites start below 10, DR 30 to 50 is solid for a growing business, 50 to 70 is well established, and 70 and above is for major brands and publishers. Compare yourself with competitors in your niche rather than with the whole web.

How is Domain Rating calculated?

Ahrefs looks at how many unique websites link to a site with followed links, how strong those linking websites are, and how many other sites each of them links to. The result is placed on a 0 to 100 scale that gets harder to climb the higher you go.

How can I increase my DR?

Earn followed links from more unique, relevant and strong websites: publish content people want to cite, create free tools or data, write guest posts, get listed in quality directories, and keep the links you already have.

Is Domain Rating the same as Domain Authority?

No. Domain Rating is Ahrefs’ score and Domain Authority (DA) is Moz’s. Both estimate the strength of a site’s backlinks on a 0 to 100 scale, but they use different link data and formulas, so the numbers differ.

Does Domain Rating affect Google rankings?

Google does not use DR. It is a third-party estimate. But the thing DR measures, strong links from other websites, is something Google does value, so a rising DR usually goes with growing authority.
